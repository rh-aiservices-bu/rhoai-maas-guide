= Fed Aura Capital: The Enterprise AI Gateway Scenario

A bank, a CIO mandate, and six fake models. This page is the production guide for the {maas} 3.6 product video: how the video is cut, what is said over it, the artwork it needs, and - after the curtain - exactly what was built on the cluster to make every frame possible.

The story: Fed Aura Capital, a bank with 80,000 employees, decides to kill shadow AI. No more personal Claude subscriptions, no more ad-hoc Cursor keys, no more Gemini in a browser tab. Every employee, every application, every agent reaches every model through one gate - the Red Hat AI Gateway, powered by {maas}. What each department sees, and how much it can spend, is decided by policy, not code.

This document is ordered from lowest complexity to highest. First the video, then the words over the video, then the artwork, then the demo environment that makes it all true.

TIP: All file paths and `oc apply` commands in this guide are relative to the link:{repo-url}[rhoai-maas-guide] repository root. Make sure you have cloned it and are working from its root directory (see xref:index.adoc#_getting_started[Getting Started]).

== The video

*Target: ~4:35, ceiling 5:00. Two presenters in role, in discussion - the CIO's mandate voice and the platform engineer's proof voice. Not voice-of-god narration. Screen recordings of the live environment on a real cluster; no re-enactments of the UI.*

The video runs six scenes. Each scene lists what is on screen, what the cut should feel like, and who carries the dialogue (see the talk track).

=== Scene 1 - The bank and the mandate (0:00-0:45)

On screen: the Fed Aura Capital org chart (asset M3) full-screen at open - 80,000 employees, seven divisions. Mid-scene, a 3-second B-roll flash of the shadow AI being killed - a browser chatbot tab, a personal provider in an IDE - stamped with the CIO email (asset M1) as the verdict.

The scale is the hook. Open on the number, not on prose: 80,000 people, seven divisions, every one of them already reaching for AI. The mandate lands in dialogue; the email flash is the evidence, not the opening.

=== Scene 2 - One gate for everything (0:45-1:15)

On screen: the architecture diagram (asset M2): 80,000 employees on the left, the Red Hat AI Gateway in the middle, the two model estates on the right - on-prem models on the company's own GPUs, and cloud/SaaS models (general, large-context, image generation) in external clouds.

The engineer owns this beat: one gateway in front of everything, and nothing bypasses it. The CIO lands the "single pane of glass" line.

=== Scene 3 - The catalog is the policy (1:15-1:55)

On screen: the {rhoai} Dashboard model catalog. One full login as a developer, then a three-way split-screen card (~8s): the developer's catalog (coding models + general purpose), the marketer's (general purpose + image model), the credit officer's (one on-prem general model).

One login establishes the mechanic; the split screen carries the comparison. No repeated logins - login cycles are dead air.

The platform hides what a user cannot access. No "request access" button, no greyed-out rows. The catalog is the policy.

=== Scene 4 - The admin view (1:55-2:45)

On screen: admin side of the dashboard. Two cuts:

* The subscription list - one per division, human-readable names, visible per-model hourly limits, and a description of what the tier is for.
* The usage dashboard - consumption per model across the bank. Tokens first; dollars where the platform reports model pricing (see the xref:#the_dollar_display[branch] below).

The CIO owns this beat: caps encode the cost model, on-prem is sunk cost, cloud is rented - and spend is visible in dollars.

=== Scene 5 - The developer's day (2:45-3:15)

On screen: split screen, one cut, ~30 seconds. Left: the {rhoai} Dashboard endpoints page - model name, base URL, the freshly generated API key. Right: a VS Code workbench with the Zoo Code extension.

Copy URL and key, add the "Red Hat AI" OpenAI-compatible provider, the dropdown populates with only the division's allowed models, the token meter climbs in real time - input and output counted. No hello world on camera: the populated dropdown and the live meter are the proof.

This is the payoff: the whole integration is copy-and-paste. The developer never sees a cluster.

=== Scene 6 - Guardrails and the scoreboard (3:15-4:35)

On screen: the "55 minutes later..." cut card (asset M5), then the IT admin mid-generation: the request is rate-limited on the cloud model. The dropdown switches to the on-prem Opus 5.1 and generation continues, uninterrupted.

Then the closing beat, and the frame the video ends on: the usage dashboard across all seven divisions - tokens and dollars. The scoreboard: who is using what, what it costs, one gate.

The video ends on the mandate's promise. Closing line: "One gateway. Policy decides. The work keeps moving."

== Talk track

Two presenters in role, in discussion - not narrators reading lines:

* *CIO (Erwan)* - carries the why: the mandate, the cost model, the policy.
* *Platform engineer (Roberto)* - carries the how: enforcement, integration, proof.

Total dialogue budget: ~5 minutes, ~700 spoken words. Lines below are cues, not a verbatim script; keep the exchanges natural. The role assignment is swappable.

=== Scene 1 - The bank and the mandate

*CIO:* 80,000 people. Seven divisions. Last quarter, every one of them had an AI subscription we paid for and could not see.

*Engineer:* So no memo. One email - starting next month, every AI conversation in this bank goes through one gateway.

*CIO:* Who sees what - and what they can spend - lives in one place I control. Not buried in application code nobody can audit.

=== Scene 2 - One gate for everything

*Engineer:* The whole architecture. On the left, the bank - all users, apps, and agents. On the right, two estates: models we own on our own GPUs, and models we rent from external clouds.

*CIO:* And in the middle, the only path. One place to see, control, and bill for all AI usage.

*Engineer:* Nothing reaches a model without passing through it.

=== Scene 3 - The catalog is the policy

*Engineer:* Watch the policy become visible. I log in as a developer: the coding models plus general purpose. Nothing else exists from this seat.

*CIO:* A marketer sees the public general model plus the image model. A credit officer gets one on-prem general model - customer data never leaves. The policy is per division, and it lives in the platform, not in a wiki page.

*Engineer:* No greyed-out rows, no request-access button. The catalog is the policy - and the same login that opens the dashboard authorizes every API call.

=== Scene 4 - The admin view

*CIO:* My view. Each division has one subscription with per-model hourly caps. The caps encode the cost model, not the technology.

*Engineer:* On-prem is a sunk cost - we own the GPUs - so those caps are generous. Cloud is rented, so they are tight. IT runs the platform, so it gets roughly five times the standard caps.

*CIO:* And spend shows in dollars, not just tokens.

=== Scene 5 - The developer's day

*Engineer:* Integration is copy-and-paste. Base URL, API key, done. The dropdown only lists what the division is allowed - the catalog follows them into the IDE.

*CIO:* And the meter is live. The gateway counts every token of every request.

=== Scene 6 - Guardrails and the scoreboard

*Engineer:* Fifty-five minutes of real work later: the cloud model's hourly budget is gone. That 429 is earned, not staged.

*CIO:* So the work moves. On-prem is sunk cost - the fallback has room to run.

*Engineer:* (switches the dropdown to Opus 5.1; generation continues)

*CIO:* (on the scoreboard) One gateway. Policy decides. The work keeps moving.

== The demo environment

This is what it took to build the thing: a fully working, CPU-only replica of the Fed Aura Capital setup. No real models, no GPUs - every "model" is the llm-d inference simulator with a convincing name. The manifests in this repo apply the exact same governance a customer would configure; a real deployment swaps the simulator containers for real serving stacks and leaves every policy, subscription, and user untouched.

=== Prerequisites

* {maas} installed through xref:05-maas-models.adoc[Phase 5] of this guide
* `oc` logged in as cluster-admin
* `htpasswd`, `curl`, `jq` on PATH
* ~200m CPU and 512Mi memory per model - the whole scenario runs on one CPU node

The philosophy, and the line to say out loud: *caps are a property of cost, not of capability.* On-prem models run on GPUs the bank already bought - sunk cost - so their caps are generous. Cloud models are rented per token - so their caps are tight. IT gets roughly five times the standard caps because it runs the platform and the pilots.

Each division gets ONE `MaaSSubscription` covering all of its models, one API key per user, and priority 30 so the division tier outranks any shipped default subscription (see the xref:#workshop-extensions[appendix] for how the shipped tiers interact).

=== The scripts

All under `demo/corporate-scenario/`:

|===
| Script | What it does

| `setup-demo.sh`
| Builds the whole environment: 14 users, 7 groups, the `htpasswd` identity provider, the `cloud-models` namespace, all six LLMInferenceServices, and the MaaS CRDs (model refs, auth policies, subscriptions). `DEMO_PASSWORD='...'` skips the interactive prompt.

| `run-demo.sh`
| Walks the video beats from the CLI: per-division catalog visibility, one inference request per allowed model, and prints the exact base URL + API key the IDE scene needs (scene 5).
| `warmup-cloud-quota.sh`
| Burns down the cloud model's hourly quota until the next rate-limit window boundary. This is what makes scene 6 true: the 429 on camera is a real, earned exhaustion of a real cap.

| `verify-fa-cap.sh`
| The Fed Aura Capital test suite: the full 7x6 access matrix (allow/deny per cell), rate-limit behavior on the cloud models, key multiplication (limits are per subscription, not per credential), and config-drift checks that subscription caps match the matrix above.

| `cleanup-demo.sh`
| Tears everything down - CRDs, services, namespace, groups, users, identity provider. Nothing from the base guide is touched.
|===

Setup, then the video sequence:

[source,bash]
----
cd demo/corporate-scenario
./setup-demo.sh

# the video, in order:
./run-demo.sh                 # scenes 3-5 (catalogs, admin view, IDE config)
./warmup-cloud-quota.sh       # prepare scene 6 before the "55 minutes later" cut
./verify-fa-cap.sh            # prove the whole matrix holds
./cleanup-demo.sh             # when done
----

NOTE: The oauth pods restart after the identity provider is added. Wait about a minute before logging in as the demo users.

=== What gets created

# Auth policies - one per division

# Simulator pods, both estates
oc get pods -n llm -l app.kubernetes.io/part-of=llminferenceservice
oc get pods -n cloud-models -l app.kubernetes.io/part-of=llminferenceservice

== Workshop extensions (not in the video)

For live workshops where the audience gets their hands in. None of this appears in the 5-minute cut; it is here so the same environment can carry a longer session.

=== Prove the denial

The video hides unauthorized models; a workshop can also prove the enforcement. As `sales-1`, call the on-prem code model endpoint directly:

[source,bash]
----
curl -s -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}\n' -H "Authorization: Bearer $SALES_KEY" \
  https://maas.<domain>/llm/opus-5-1/v1/chat/completions
# 403
----

Hidden in the catalog, denied at the API - the same policy, two faces.

=== Change a limit live

Governance is policy, not deployment:

[source,bash]
----
oc patch maassubscription fedauro-developers -n models-as-a-service --type=json \
  -p '[{"op":"replace","path":"/spec/modelRefs/1/tokenRateLimits/0/limit","value":10000000}]'
----

Re-run `./run-demo.sh` - the new cap is in effect immediately, no redeploy. Restore it the same way.

=== Key multiplication does not help

As `dev-1`, mint two keys and exhaust the shared quota with key one: key two is rate-limited the instant key one is. The limit is per subscription, not per credential. (`verify-fa-cap.sh` runs this automatically.)
